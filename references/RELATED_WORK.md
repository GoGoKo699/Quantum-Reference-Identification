# Related work and attribution

This account concerns one promised-match lookup in an independently Haar-distributed library: there are M labels, m unknown pure reference copies per label in dimension d, and t copies of the uniformly selected reference as the query. The proposed contribution is the paired growing-library quantum error law and sharp classical-preprocessing saturation. General quantum-memory advantages, measurement chronology, symmetric-subspace identities, state-comparison tests and general discrimination inequalities are established ingredients.

## Programmable identification and quantum matching

[Hayashi, Horibe and Hashimoto (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.72.052306) solve binary Haar-average pure-state identification in arbitrary dimension with multiple reference copies and one query copy. [He and Bergou (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.75.032316), [Sentís et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.82.042312), and [Zhou et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.26421/QIC12.11-12-9) develop multicopy programmable discrimination. Zhou et al.'s published Eqs. (35) and (39) give the arbitrary-dimension binary minimum-error optimum with arbitrary program/data counts. The binary inverse-capacity error scale and Haar/symmetric-projector reduction are therefore inherited. This project does not improve that binary optimum.

The additional finite-query statement controls accumulation of error as M grows, including the small-error regime, through the second and third moments of the matching ensemble. A reduction to one binary pair alone does not establish that M dependence. The PGM, generalized Holevo–Curlander inequality and sequential-projector union bound used in its proof are inherited from the discrimination literature, including [Tyson (2009), Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2094) and [Gao (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.92.052331). The contribution claimed here is their evaluated consequence for this ensemble.

[Sasaki and Carlini (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.66.022303) already compare fully quantum and semiclassical matching machines supplied with unknown templates. Their solved setting uses two equatorial qubit templates and a fidelity-based matching score without the exact-membership promise. [Gambs (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0444) already discusses multiclass quantum classification, swap-test comparisons and copy budgets; some of its reductions supply a Helstrom-learning oracle. Neither the multiclass task nor comparing retained quantum templates with a classical learning record is a new concept here. Our resource statement supplies and charges the actual unknown-state copies, memory, sequential scan and gate precision; it does not assume a free trained discrimination oracle.

## Classical learning records and later information

[Sentís, Calsamiglia, Muñoz-Tapia and Bagan (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00708) construct a binary pure-qubit learning machine whose classical record matches the fully quantum programmable discriminator when a single later query qubit is supplied. Collective learning can be strictly better than independently estimating the references. Consequently our classical comparator permits arbitrary collective measurements of all references, with unlimited classical records and computation. It is not restricted to tomography or independent measurements. Our separation has different quantifiers: a growing number of classes and even an exact later query description.

The information-arrival order belongs to the established framework of [Ballester, Wehner and Winter (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2008.928276) and [Gopal and Wehner (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.82.022326). A receiver measures before side information arrives, retains a bounded quantum memory, and later guesses the unrevealed label. Gopal and Wehner also supply general power bounds; the use of moments here is not a new general discrimination method. The surviving statement is the sharp leading optimum for the planted Haar-reference library with fixed positive m: classicalizing the references before learning an exact later query leaves success asymptotic to (log M)^m/(m! M) when M grows and d/log M diverges. The inspected MUB/Clifford examples do not state this evaluation. This scoped comparison is not an exhaustive priority certificate.

The auxiliary finite-query classical ceiling comes from established positive channel structure. [Chiribella (2010), Eqs. (6)–(7)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1875), decomposes universal measure-and-prepare estimation channels into loss and cloning terms. Its state-independent term gives the common-component weight used here. [Anshu, Landau and Liu (2022), Lemma 7 and Theorem 9](https://doi.org/10.1145/3519935.3519974), already exploit this mechanism in distributed quantum inner-product estimation, including separable-measurement lower bounds. The resulting finite-query bound of order 1/M+mt/d is a credited corollary, not the central new result.

## Classical sketches and the reference budget

[Zhang and Heidari (2025)](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2025.16) construct reusable classical quantum-data sketches and search indexes. Their Corollaries 14 and 16 build empirical sketches from order d/epsilon^2 copies per state; Theorem 17 gives sublinear classical lookup after preprocessing. Their goal treats initialization samples differently from subsequent storage and lookup. Here the scarce reference budget itself is constrained and only one lookup is guaranteed. We claim neither their reusable index nor a faster database scan.

[Huang, Kueng and Preskill (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0932-7) already allow observables to be chosen after classical-shadow measurements. Global-Clifford shadows, applied to a later known rank-one query projector and amplified across the labels, supply an O(log M)-reference-copy comparator at fixed accuracy for a separated library. Thus the fixed-m saturation is a success-probability separation under the same training budget. It is not an exponential reference-sample separation when the budget may grow.

## Bounded comparison gap

[Li (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.86.032320) studies bounds for unambiguous discrimination and explicitly applies them to identification of several unknown multicopy qudit states. Its primary abstract was verified, but its full text remained subscription restricted on this review. Unambiguous identification allows an inconclusive result and differs from the minimum-error objective used here. We therefore retain this specific source-access limitation and make no exhaustive literature-clearance claim. The sharp classical exact-query theorem does not follow from the verified abstract.
